# Product Discovery Questions

> **Status:** Interview guide for pilot and customer discovery — not a sales script.

Use these open-ended questions during hotel discovery interviews. Questions are organized by role to explore problems documented in [Hotel Problems](HOTEL_PROBLEMS.md).

Prioritize interviews with the approved initial discovery segment: **independent mid-size hotels with meaningful cross-department operational complexity**. See [Target Customer](TARGET_CUSTOMER.md). Other segments may be interviewed for contrast; they are not the discovery center.

Goal: validate or invalidate hypotheses—not pitch HuGuWeb features.

---

## Interview Principles

- Ask about **current behavior**, **workarounds**, and **impact**
- Follow up with "Can you tell me more about that?" and "What happens next?"
- Avoid revealing feature priorities until late in the conversation if needed
- Record evidence level per finding using [Evidence Model](EVIDENCE_MODEL.md)

---

## General Manager

Explore operational visibility, reporting, system fragmentation, approval bottlenecks, and cost visibility.

1. Walk me through how you start your day. What do you check first to understand how the property is running?
2. When something goes wrong operationally, how do you usually find out about it?
3. What information do you wish you had sooner than you currently get it?
4. How do you review performance across departments today? What tools or reports do you use?
5. Where do you see the most delays waiting for someone else's approval or decision?
6. How do you track costs that affect profitability (labor, inventory, maintenance, discounts)?
7. Which systems does your team use daily? Where do you see duplicate work between them?
8. If you could fix one operational frustration this month, what would it be?
9. How do you prepare for high-occupancy periods or large groups? What breaks first?
10. What would need to be true for you to consider changing your core operational software?

---

## Front Office

Explore reservation handoffs, room readiness, guest information, check-in/check-out friction, and housekeeping communication.

1. How do you learn which rooms are ready for check-in on a busy arrival day?
2. What typically causes check-in delays? What do you do when that happens?
3. How do reservation changes (late arrival, room type, special requests) reach you today?
4. When housekeeping or maintenance updates a room status, how do you find out?
5. How do you handle guest information that was captured at booking vs at the desk?
6. Walk me through checkout from the guest request to the room being available again.
7. How do you communicate urgent room priorities to housekeeping or maintenance?
8. What guest requests are hardest to track to completion across departments?
9. Where do you re-enter information that already exists somewhere else?
10. What do you handle in WhatsApp, paper, radio, or Excel that is not in your main system?
11. What part of the front desk workflow feels most fragile during peak hours?

---

## Housekeeping

Explore task assignment, room status, supervisor inspection, lost/late information, and how the floor currently communicates.

1. How do you currently learn which rooms need attention and in what priority?
2. How are tasks assigned to room attendants each day?
3. How do you update room status when cleaning or inspection is complete?
4. What happens when front office needs a room faster than planned?
5. When your team finds maintenance issues or missing items, what is the process?
6. How do supervisors verify quality before a room is marked ready?
7. Where does information get lost or arrive late in your workflow?
8. What tools do staff use on the floor (paper, phone, app, radio)?
9. How do you track linen, amenities, or supplies your team consumes?
10. If room status were wrong for an hour, what impact would that have on your team and guests?

---

## Purchasing / Warehouse

Explore requisitions, approvals, stock visibility, receiving, and supplier workflows.

1. How does a department request something they need to purchase or withdraw from stock?
2. Who approves purchases, and how long does approval usually take?
3. How do you know what is in stock before promising items to operations?
4. Walk me through receiving a delivery from arrival to items being usable.
5. Where do purchasing and finance systems disagree or require manual reconciliation?
6. How do you handle urgent operational requests that cannot wait for normal lead times?
7. What do you track in Excel or paper that you wish connected to daily operations?
8. How do you choose suppliers and compare prices today?
9. When stock counts differ from the system, how is that investigated?
10. Which departments create the most friction or exceptions in your process?
11. If inventory or purchasing software were unavailable for a busy week, what would actually break in daily hotel operations—and what would you handle on paper anyway?

---

## HR

Explore leave, overtime, payroll access, staff transportation, training, and accommodation.

1. How do employees request leave or report overtime today?
2. How do department managers learn about staffing availability for their shifts?
3. What HR information do managers need from operations, and how do they get it?
4. How does payroll data get prepared each period? What is manual?
5. If applicable: how are staff transportation or accommodation requests handled?
6. How do you track training or certification requirements for staff?
7. Where do HR and department schedules fall out of sync?
8. What employee self-service exists today, if any?
9. What HR-related issues create operational problems on the property floor?
10. Which HR workflows cause the most repeated administrative work?

---

## Technical Service / Maintenance

Explore maintenance requests, preventive maintenance, assets, task assignment, and failure history.

1. How does a maintenance request get created from a guest or staff report?
2. How do you prioritize work orders when multiple issues are open?
3. How does front office or housekeeping learn a room is blocked or released?
4. Do you run preventive maintenance schedules? How are they tracked?
5. How do you record what was done on an asset or in a room over time?
6. Where do you source parts or materials for repairs?
7. What causes the longest delays in closing maintenance tasks?
8. Which failures repeat because root cause history is hard to access?
9. How do external contractors fit into your workflow?
10. What maintenance information would help other departments if they had it sooner?

---

## Food & Beverage (if applicable)

Explore POS, folio posting, inventory, and event coordination.

1. How do F&B charges reach a guest folio or payment today?
2. How do banquet or event details flow from sales/reservations to the kitchen?
3. How do you track food cost or waste in relation to daily operations?
4. Where does F&B depend on systems outside the PMS?
5. What F&B operational handoffs fail most often during busy service?

---

## Finance / Accounting (optional depth)

Explore how money moves today. Do not pitch HuGuWeb as accounting software. Distinguish operational events from the hotel’s books.

1. How does folio or operational data reach your accounting system today?
2. What reconciliations are manual at month-end?
3. Which numbers does operations need during the day vs only at month-end?
4. Which compliance reports (e.g., e-invoice) create the most operational friction?
5. Where do finance and operations disagree on numbers?
6. What is handled in Excel because the PMS and accounting system do not connect?

---

## Cross-Role Closing Questions

1. Which handoffs between departments fail most often?
2. What workarounds exist because systems do not connect?
3. What would you never give up from your current tools?
4. Who else should we talk to on your team to understand this better?

---

## Questions NOT to Ask

Avoid biased, leading, or feature-selling questions such as:

| Do not ask | Why |
|------------|-----|
| "Would you use our amazing mobile housekeeping app?" | Assumes solution; leads the witness |
| "Don't you hate your current PMS?" | Leading and adversarial |
| "We can replace your ERP—would that excite you?" | Oversells scope not validated |
| "You need integrated inventory, right?" | Assumes pain and priority |
| "How much would you pay for an all-in-one platform?" | Pricing discovery too early without problem depth |
| "Would you switch if we had channel manager built-in?" | Feature checklist mentality |
| "Isn't Excel terrible for everything?" | Leading; may shut down nuanced answers |
| "Our Hotel Operating System will connect everything—interested?" | Pitch language; not discovery |
| "You want AI reporting, don't you?" | Invented priority |
| "Which of these 20 features matter most?" | Forces feature ranking before problem understanding |

Prefer neutral phrasing:

- "How do you handle that today?"
- "What happens when that fails?"
- "Who is involved in that process?"
- "How often does that occur?"
